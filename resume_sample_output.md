## 3. Pivot Positioning Notes

**(a) What to lead with and emphasize:**
The renewal ownership on your top 5 accounts is your single strongest asset here — two years of doing the actual work of this role, unofficially, is far more credible to a hiring manager than a title alone would be. Your 94% forecast accuracy and VP/C-level relationship-building are the supporting evidence that you operate with the discipline and stakeholder trust a renewal-owning role actually requires.

**(b) What to minimize or reframe (not hide):**
Don't lead with the $4.2M new-ARR / 118%-of-quota framing — it's real and worth keeping, but leading with a "hunter" number signals a new-business-first identity, which is the opposite of what this posting wants. Reframe it as supporting evidence of relationship depth (see the rewritten bullet above) rather than the headline. The DataFlow "trained 4 new hires" bullet is sales-team enablement — genuinely less relevant here; consider dropping it from a tailored version of your resume rather than working to fit it in.

**(c) Skill or credential gaps genuinely worth naming:**
"Drive product adoption" is a real, specific gap — nothing in your background speaks to usage metrics, onboarding, or customer health scoring. This isn't something to paper over in the resume; be ready to speak to it directly in an interview (e.g., how relationship management and forecasting discipline transfer to adoption tracking, even without direct prior ownership of it). No certification gap stands out as necessary given the strength of your practical experience.

**(d) An honest answer for "why this move":**
*"I've effectively been doing the retention work of this role for two years — I own the renewal conversations on my five biggest accounts because the relationships already ran through me, not because it was in my job description. I want to do that work with the actual mandate, metrics, and team behind it, instead of it being an unofficial add-on to a quota-carrying sales role."*

>

That's already how I operate today, informally. I'd welcome the chance to talk about how that experience translates directly into what you're building on this team.
>
